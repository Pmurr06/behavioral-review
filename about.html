About The Behavioral Review

Our Mission

The Behavioral Review was founded to ensure that meaningful undergraduate work does not disappear into a gradebook. We provide students with a permanent place to publish original scholarship, strengthen their writing through constructive editorial feedback, and build a body of work they can use for graduate school, law school, internships, and future careers.

We believe undergraduate scholarship represents serious intellectual work. Student authors deserve an accessible publication that respects their ideas, preserves their individual voice, and helps their strongest work reach readers beyond the classroom.

About the Publication

The Behavioral Review is a student-led, editor-reviewed undergraduate publication featuring scholarship across the behavioral sciences, social sciences, humanities, business, health, and interdisciplinary fields.

We publish original research, literature reviews, analytical essays, case studies, policy analysis, evidence-based commentary, and other forms of academic work from undergraduate authors.

Our contributors represent different universities, disciplines, and academic backgrounds. We are building a publication where thoughtful undergraduate scholarship can receive serious editorial attention and remain permanently accessible online.

Editorial Philosophy

Our editorial process is designed to strengthen each submission without replacing the author's voice. Editors may improve clarity, organization, readability, sourcing, and presentation while preserving the student's central argument and individual writing style.

Our primary editorial focus includes:

  • Behavioral Science
  • Psychology
  • Sociology
  • Criminology & Criminal Justice
  • Business & Economics
  • Public Policy
  • Political Science
  • International Affairs
  • Health Sciences

We also consider closely related disciplines that align with our editorial mission.

For complete submission requirements and accepted formats, visit the For Authors page.

Editorial Standards

Originality

We publish original research, analysis, commentary, and academic work not previously published elsewhere.

Evidence and Support

Claims should be supported by credible evidence, relevant scholarship, sound reasoning, or clearly explained analysis appropriate to the subject.

Relevance

Submissions should address a meaningful academic, social, cultural, scientific, legal, economic, or professional question.

Clarity

Writing should be organized, readable, and accessible to an educated undergraduate audience.

Author Voice

Editorial revisions should strengthen the work without replacing the author's perspective or individual style.

Academic Integrity

Sources must be represented honestly, citations must be accurate, and submitted work must meet our originality and plagiarism standards.
